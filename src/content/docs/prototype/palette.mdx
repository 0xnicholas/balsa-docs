The three candidates differ only in **hue**: amber (36°), bronze (28°), clay (18°). Saturation
and lightness are tuned so the hard pairs clear WCAG AA — in light mode deliberately with little
headroom, because a warm accent that is *safe* enough to pass is close to one that has stopped
looking amber.

Each block shows light and dark for the same palette, so the pairing can be judged together:
the light accent is a deep wood tone, the dark accent a pale sand — they are different colours,
they just share a hue.

<PaletteGrid />

## What to look at

- **Body text and headings** — the neutral greys carry the warmth at the "warm neutrals" depth
  rung. At the token-only rung they stay cold, and the warm accent has to do all the work.
- **The link colour** — the tightest pair on the page. Its ratio is printed under each block.
- **The chip and the primary button** — where `accent-low` and `accent` do their real work.
- **The table hairlines** — where a warm neutral scale is most visible, and most at risk of
  looking muddy.

Flip the switcher at the bottom of the screen (`←` `→` for palette, `↑` `↓` for depth) to see the
same pages with a different pairing live.
